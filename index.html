Open this file in Safari (not Files preview)

When the top banner turns green and says “JavaScript running”, you’re good. If it’s red, the page is rendering inside the iOS Files app preview, which doesn’t execute JavaScript. Tap the Share icon in the upper right of the preview and pick Open in Safari. Or long-press the file in Files → Share → Safari.

Step 1

Take or upload a photo

A full-face selfie works great — you’ll pick which eye to analyze in the next step. Close-up iris photos also work. Shoot in soft daylight, avoid on-camera flash if possible.

By using eyeD, your iris photo may be used to help build and improve the Davis Iris Color System. No photos are shared publicly or sold.

Step 2

Tap on the eye you want to analyze

We’ll zoom in and fit the iris ring automatically on the next step.

Tap the center of the iris. Use the slider to adjust the crop.
Step 3 · —

Fit the donut to the iris

Tap to center. Sliders size the iris ring and pupil exclusion. Flash reflections and eyelids are filtered out automatically.

What this prototype proves

A classical-CV iris analyzer running entirely in the browser. Full-face capture, tap to locate either eye, zoom and fit, and a Davis Card per eye — no server, no ML, no photo leaves your phone.

Production path

This spec ports to native iOS (SwiftUI + Vision + Core Image). Native adds automatic eye detection via Vision’s face-landmarks, real-time iris auto-centering, persistent eye-pair library, MP4 reveal export, and opted-in dataset collection for the v1.1 Core ML pattern classifier (Rayid Flower / Stream / Shaker / Jewel).
